# Privacy and local-network notes

The demo uses fictional seeded identities and stores account, programme, attendance, messages, and case records in a local SQLite database under `data/`. Uploaded audio/recordings and generated certificates are stored under `data/` too. Those files are excluded from Git. This is local storage on the machine running the server, not browser-only storage or cloud sync.

Voice, live video, recording playback, and QR scanning may request camera or microphone access only after a user starts the relevant action. Live-session recording is optional and off by default. Speech/translation inference is local when the optional models are installed. If local transcription is unavailable, a participant may explicitly choose browser speech recognition; that audio is processed by the browser's configured speech provider and may leave the Voxera server. Live automatic captions also use the browser's configured speech provider and may process audio externally; they are opt-in and separate from saving the video. Captions generated for uploaded videos use the optional local Whisper model and FFmpeg, without sending media to a hosted service. Hosted Voxera AI is separately configured by the server operator and requires each participant's explicit **Allow external AI for text answers** consent. When enabled, confirmed text and limited account/programme context for that participant may be sent to the configured provider. The assistant never sends voice audio to that provider. Turning remote AI consent off prevents subsequent requests from using that service; it does not revoke data already processed by the provider. Review the provider's retention and privacy terms before enabling this feature.

## Account and participant data

Participant registration creates a globally unique account using a normalized email/username, name, bcrypt password hash, preferred language, and optional area. A participant is connected to an organisation through a programme application/enrolment; the participant account is not duplicated for each organisation. Organisers belong to the organisation workspace created with their organiser account. Programme conversations and access to participant records are scoped to the relevant programme and organisation.

The SQLite database also stores programme applications and status changes, session attendance and progress, programme messages and read timestamps, live-session chat messages, recording comments, follow-up case text and replies, consent choices, certificates, and audit events. Live chat is available to organisers and currently enrolled participants while the live session is active; session schedules, recording comments, and playback are available to the owning organisation and enrolled or completed participants. Applicants and waitlisted participants cannot access sessions or recordings until enrolled. Organisers choose whether each recording expires at the session deadline or remains available without an expiry date; no-expiry does not bypass enrolment access checks. Voice files are retained only when the participant has enabled voice-storage consent; otherwise audio is removed after processing. Programme cover images are optional published programme content, limited to PNG, JPEG, or WebP and 5 MB, and are stored under `data/uploads/`.

On startup, existing follow-up cases and support conversations without a programme link are associated only when the participant had exactly one qualifying programme at the time. Ambiguous records are left unchanged rather than guessed into another organisation's inbox.

The browser keeps the signed-in bearer token in local storage. Standard tokens expire after 12 hours; platform owner tokens expire after one hour. Signing out removes the browser copy. Passwords are stored as bcrypt hashes, not plaintext. The platform owner is provisioned only through the local `python -m backend.manage_owner` command and signs in through a separate, explicit owner entry. The owner can view account email addresses, account creation and successful sign-in timestamps, and audit events; directory access and owner changes are themselves audited. The owner assistant uses aggregate counts only and does not inspect private messages or use a hosted AI service. An operator may optionally configure `VOXERA_OWNER_OLLAMA_MODEL` to use a local Ollama model; only aggregate platform counts and the owner's question are sent to that local model. Blocking prevents future authenticated access. Organisation archiving preserves programme and participant history; participant deletion is permanent, requires owner password re-entry and exact email confirmation, and removes linked participant records. Active live classes prevent organisation archiving or programme blocking until organisers end them normally. There is no automated data-retention schedule, self-service account deletion, backup/restore workflow, or production-grade account recovery yet. Protect the whole `data/` directory and use fictional records until those operational controls and the production deployment are reviewed.

The platform owner console does not grant silent live-class access, record hidden presence, or bypass enrolment checks. Organisers and participants retain the normal role and programme access boundaries.

For camera/microphone access from Android, use HTTPS. Install `mkcert`, then from the project directory:

```powershell
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force certs
mkcert -install
mkcert -cert-file certs/voxera.pem -key-file certs/voxera-key.pem localhost 127.0.0.1 YOUR_LAPTOP_LAN_IP
```

Trust the mkcert root CA on the Android device, run `start.ps1`, and open `https://YOUR_LAPTOP_LAN_IP:4173` on the same Wi-Fi. Keep certificates private and do not expose the demo server to the public internet. `data/` contains personal-data-like demo content and should be protected accordingly.
